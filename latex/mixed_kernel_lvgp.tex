\section{Motivation (what the 30-seed study showed)}

Across the study's replicated benchmarks the Categorical GP (botorch
\texttt{MixedSingleTaskGP}) beat the latent-variable H-LVGP in most configurations. Two
structural mechanisms were isolated:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The offset tax.} The LVGP has a single product kernel and one constant mean, so
a pure between-level offset can only be encoded by pushing latent vectors apart --- which
decays the correlation for \emph{everything}, offsets and shape alike (demonstrated by the
Griewank $b(\ell)$ redesign, which flipped every budget to the Categorical GP).
\item \textbf{The additive channel.} botorch's mixed kernel contains an \emph{additive}
categorical component, equivalent to a per-level random-effect offset $h(\ell)$ carried in the
covariance: $f(x,\ell)=g(x)+h(\ell)+\text{interaction}$. This absorbs offsets at the cost of a
single outputscale.
\end{enumerate}
The mixed-kernel H-LVGP grafts botorch's kernel algebra onto the LVGP: the latent kernel takes
the categorical slot, so the model keeps the interpretable latent embedding \emph{and} gains
the offset channel. Notably, its level random effects are correlated through latent distance
--- similar levels share offsets --- which even the Categorical GP cannot express (its offsets
are exchangeable).

\section{The botorch reference implementation}

\texttt{botorch v0.14.0}, \texttt{models/gp\_regression\_mixed.py} (L109--152) builds, for
continuous dims $x$ and categorical dims $c$:
\begin{align}
K_{\mathrm{sum}} &= \sigma_s^2\Big(k_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(1)}(x,x') \;+\;
      \sigma_c^2\, k_{\mathrm{cat}}^{(1)}(c,c')\Big) \tag{L124--138}\\
K_{\mathrm{prod}} &= \sigma_p^2\; k_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(2)}(x,x')\cdot
      k_{\mathrm{cat}}^{(2)}(c,c') \tag{L139--151}\\
K &= K_{\mathrm{sum}} + K_{\mathrm{prod}} \qquad\text{(no global scale)} \tag{L152}
\end{align}
with the following exact ingredients:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Independent instances} for the sum and product terms (class docstring L41--43:
``we fit different lengthscales for the kernels in the sum and product terms'').
\item \textbf{Continuous kernels}: ARD RBF from
\texttt{get\_covar\_module\_with\_dim\_scaled\_prior}\\
(\texttt{models/utils/gpytorch\_modules.py} L100--133): lengthscale prior
$\ell_i \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}\!\big(\sqrt2 + \tfrac12\ln d,\ \sqrt3\big)$
(the Hvarfner-2024 dimension-scaled prior, assuming unit-cube inputs), hard floor
$\ell_i \ge 0.025$ via \texttt{GreaterThan(0.025, transform=None,
initial\_value=prior.mode)}.
\item \textbf{Categorical kernels}: $k_{\mathrm{cat}}(c,c') = \exp(-d_H(c,c')/\ell_c)$ with
Hamming distance $d_H$, per-dim $\ell_c \ge 10^{-6}$, \emph{no prior}
(\texttt{models/kernels/categorical.py}).
\item \textbf{Scales}: the inner categorical term of the sum part carries its own
\texttt{ScaleKernel} (L130); both parts carry outer \texttt{ScaleKernel}s; none carry priors.
\item Mean: \texttt{ConstantMean}; outcome standardized (\texttt{Standardize}); known
observation noise via \texttt{train\_Yvar}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The Categorical GP's kernel, precisely}
The kernel the study's Categorical GP uses in the categorical slots is botorch's
\texttt{CategoricalKernel} (\texttt{models/kernels/categorical.py}):
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{cat}}(c, c') \;=\; \exp\!\Big(-\,\frac{d_H(c,c')}{\ell_c}\Big),
\qquad d_H(c,c') = \mathbb 1[c \neq c']
\label{eq:hamming}
\end{equation}
(averaged over categorical dims when there are several; here there is one). Two properties
matter: (i) it is \emph{exchangeable} --- $d_H$ gives every pair of distinct levels the same
distance, so one lengthscale $\ell_c$ sets a single common between-level correlation
$e^{-1/\ell_c}$; (ii) it is of the form $\exp(-d(\cdot,\cdot))$ for a valid metric $d$, which
is what makes it positive semi-definite.

\section{The adaptation: latent kernel in the categorical slot}

The LVGP embeds level $\ell$ as $\mathbf z_\ell \in \mathbb R^2$ (lvgp-bayes
\texttt{LVMapping}) and evaluates all kernels on the embedded input
$u = [\mathbf z,\, x]$. The mixed-kernel LVGP (\texttt{LVGPRMixed}) is
\begin{align}
K\big((x,\ell),(x',\ell')\big) \;=\;
  &\ \sigma_s^2\Big( k_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(1)}(x,x')
   \;+\; \sigma_z^2\, e^{-\|\mathbf z_\ell-\mathbf z_{\ell'}\|^2 / (2\ell_z^2)} \Big) \notag\\
 +&\ \sigma_p^2\;\, k_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(2)}(x,x')\;
   e^{-\|\mathbf z_\ell-\mathbf z_{\ell'}\|^2 / 2}
\label{eq:mixedlvgp}
\end{align}
i.e.\ botorch's exact algebra with each \texttt{CategoricalKernel} replaced by an RBF over the
shared latent embedding:

\subsection*{Where the latent-slot RBF comes from, and the role of its lengthscales}

\textbf{Provenance of the RBF structure.} The RBF-over-latents is not our invention --- it is
the original LVGP correlation of Zhang et al.\ (2019). There, the level $\ell$ is mapped to
coordinates $\mathbf z_\ell \in \mathbb R^2$ and the \emph{same Gaussian correlation used for
continuous inputs} is applied to those coordinates:
\begin{equation}
r\big((x,\ell),(x',\ell')\big) \;=\;
\exp\!\Big(-\textstyle\sum_i \theta_i (x_i - x_i')^2 \;-\; \|\mathbf z_\ell -
\mathbf z_{\ell'}\|^2\Big),
\label{eq:zhang}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a product of an ARD Gaussian kernel on $x$ and a \emph{unit-lengthscale} Gaussian (RBF)
kernel on $\mathbf z$. lvgp-bayes implements exactly this: its \texttt{qual\_kernel} is an
\texttt{RBFKernel} over the embedded latent columns with lengthscale initialized to $1.0$ and
gradient frozen (\texttt{models/lvgp.py} L187--192). Validity is inherited from the RBF being
a kernel on $\mathbb R^{d_z}$: any finite set of level embeddings yields a PSD level
covariance.

\textbf{The two kernels are the same family with different metrics.} Comparing
Eq.~\ref{eq:hamming} and the latent factor of Eq.~\ref{eq:zhang}: both are
$\exp(-d(\ell,\ell')^{p}/\ell_{\mathrm{scale}})$ decays; the Categorical GP uses the
\emph{fixed, exchangeable} Hamming metric (all distinct pairs equidistant), while the LVGP
uses a \emph{learned Euclidean} metric $d(\ell,\ell') = \|\mathbf z_\ell - \mathbf z_{\ell'}\|$.
The Hamming kernel is recovered as the special case in which all latents sit at the vertices
of a regular simplex --- so replacing $k_{\mathrm{cat}}$ by the latent RBF in botorch's
algebra is a strict generalization, not a substitution of an unrelated object.

\textbf{Why the product-slot lengthscale is fixed at 1 (gauge).} In the latent RBF a
lengthscale is redundant with the scale of the embedding itself: the map $\mathbf z \mapsto
c\,\mathbf z$, $\ell_z \mapsto c\,\ell_z$ leaves the kernel invariant, so ($\mathbf z$,
$\ell_z$) are not jointly identifiable. Zhang et al.\ and lvgp-bayes resolve this by fixing
$\ell_z \equiv 1$ and letting the latent coordinates carry the scale --- a gauge choice, not a
modeling restriction. The mixed kernel keeps this gauge in the \emph{product} slot, so
$\mathbf z$ is identified exactly as in the original LVGP (latent maps remain comparable
across models).

\textbf{Why the sum-slot lengthscale is learnable.} botorch's docstring (L41--43) requires
\emph{independent} categorical lengthscales for the sum and product terms --- that freedom is
what lets the additive (offset) channel and the multiplicative (shape) channel decay at
different rates across levels. With a \emph{shared} $\mathbf z$ this freedom cannot be
absorbed into the embedding (the product slot already fixes $\mathbf z$'s scale), so the sum
slot must carry its own distance scale $\ell_z$: in Eq.~\ref{eq:mixedlvgp} the offset channel
correlates levels as $\exp(-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2/(2\ell_z^2))$. Interpretation: $\ell_z$
measures how much broader offset-sharing is than shape-sharing, in latent-distance units
(fitted example, camel $n{=}25$: $\ell_z \approx 1.7$ --- offsets shared across level
clusters whose shapes are already nearly independent).

Everything else is inherited unchanged: the latent mapping and its priors
($\mathrm{raw\ latents} \sim \mathcal N(0,\sqrt{L})$, precision $\sim$ ExpGamma), constant
mean with $\mathcal N(0,1)$ prior, internal $y$-standardization ($=$ botorch
\texttt{Standardize}), and the study wrapper's \texttt{FixedNoiseGaussianLikelihood} carrying
the replicate variances ($=$ botorch \texttt{train\_Yvar}), plus the MATLAB-parity pooled
aleatoric polynomial over $[x, \mathbf z]$ for noise-surface recovery.

\subsection*{The new kernel vs.\ the original LVGP kernel}

Writing both models with their scales makes the relationship exact. The \textbf{original
LVGP} (Zhang et al.\ 2019; \texttt{LVGPR}) is a \emph{single fully-multiplicative} channel,
\begin{equation}
K_{\mathrm{LVGP}} \;=\; \sigma^2\,
\underbrace{e^{-\sum_j \phi_j (x_j-x_j')^2}}_{\text{shape in } x}\;
\underbrace{e^{-\|\mathbf z_\ell - \mathbf z_{\ell'}\|^2}}_{\text{level similarity}},
\label{eq:lvgporig}
\end{equation}
while the \textbf{mixed kernel} (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixedlvgp}) is a sum of three channels,
$$
K_{\mathrm{mix}} = \underbrace{\sigma_s^2\, k^{(1)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}(x,x')}_{\text{(a) shared
trend}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sigma_s^2\sigma_z^2\,
e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2/(2\ell_z^2)}}_{\text{(b) level offsets}} \;+\;
\underbrace{\sigma_p^2\, k^{(2)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}(x,x')\,
e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2/2}}_{\text{(c) = the original LVGP kernel}}.
$$
\paragraph{Why and how channels (a) and (b) were added.} They were not designed
separately --- both fall out of botorch's single \emph{sum part} when it is distributed:
$$
K_{\mathrm{sum}} \;=\; \sigma_s^2\big(\,k^{(1)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}(x,x') +
\sigma_z^2\,k_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf z_\ell, \mathbf z_{\ell'})\big)
\;=\; \underbrace{\sigma_s^2\, k^{(1)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}}_{\text{(a)}} \;+\;
\underbrace{\sigma_s^2\sigma_z^2\, k_{\mathrm{lat}}}_{\text{(b)}} .
$$
The statistical reading uses the additivity property of GPs: a sum of kernels is the covariance
of a sum of \emph{independent} functions, $f \sim \mathcal{GP}(0, K_a + K_b + K_c)
\iff f = f_a + f_b + f_c$. Hence:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Channel (a) --- why:} a kernel that depends on $x$ only generates a component
$f_a(x)$ that is \emph{identical for every level} --- the common backbone all categories ride
on. The study's problems (and most physical systems with a categorical design choice) share
most of their response across levels; in the original LVGP even this shared part must survive
the latent decay $e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2}$, i.e.\ it is re-learned per level once latents
separate. \textbf{How:} an ARD RBF restricted (\texttt{active\_dims}) to the continuous
columns of the embedded input, with its own lengthscales, so the shared backbone may be
smoother or rougher than the level-specific shape in channel (c).
\item \textbf{Channel (b) --- why:} a kernel that depends on $\ell$ only generates a component
$f_b(\ell)$ that is \emph{constant in $x$} --- exactly a per-level offset, the random-effects
analogue of a per-level mean. This is the surgical answer to the offset tax diagnosed on
griewank (adding the $b(\ell)$ shifts flipped every budget to the Categorical GP): offsets now
have a dedicated route into the model that costs correlation nothing elsewhere. \textbf{How:}
the latent-slot RBF evaluated on the \emph{same} embedding $\mathbf z$ used by channel (c) ---
no new level parameters at all --- with its own distance scale $\ell_z$ and the inner
outputscale $\sigma_z^2$ (botorch L130) setting the offset-to-trend variance ratio; the outer
$\sigma_s^2$ then weighs the whole additive block against the interaction channel (c). Because
the metric is latent rather than Hamming, these offsets are \emph{correlated between similar
levels} --- the one capability here that even the Categorical GP lacks.
\end{itemize}

The differences, one by one:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The original kernel survives intact as channel (c)} --- same product form, same
unit-lengthscale latent factor, its own ARD lengthscales. The original LVGP is exactly the
special case $\sigma_s^2 = 0$: the new model \emph{nests} the old one, so it can never be
structurally worse, only harder to estimate ($q{+}4$ extra hyperparameters: one extra ARD set,
$\ell_z$, and the scale split).
\item \textbf{All-or-nothing coupling vs.\ a functional decomposition.} Under
Eq.~\ref{eq:lvgporig}, \emph{every} property of $f$ decays with the same factor
$e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2}$: if two levels sit far apart in latent space they share
\emph{nothing} --- not a global trend, not a mean shift. The mixed kernel corresponds to
$f(x,\ell) = g(x) + h(\ell) + \mathrm{int}(x,\ell)$: channel (a) is shared by \emph{all}
levels unconditionally (even latent-infinitely-separated ones), channel (b) carries mean
shifts, channel (c) carries level-specific shape. This is precisely why the original pays the
``offset tax'': a pure mean shift can only enter Eq.~\ref{eq:lvgporig} by growing
$\|\Delta\mathbf z\|$, which simultaneously destroys shape-sharing.
\item \textbf{Two latent decay rates instead of one.} The original has a single latent factor;
the mixed kernel evaluates the \emph{same} embedding at two rates ---
$e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2/2\ell_z^2}$ for offsets, $e^{-\|\Delta\mathbf z\|^2/2}$ for shapes
--- so offset-sharing can extend across level clusters whose shapes are already independent
(fitted camel example: $\ell_z\!\approx\!1.7$).
\item \textbf{Independent ARD lengthscales per channel.} The original fits one set of
$\phi_j$; the mixed kernel fits $k^{(1)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}$ and $k^{(2)}_{\mathrm{RBF}}$
separately (botorch docstring L41--43), so the shared trend may be smoother or rougher than
the level-specific shape component.
\item \textbf{Consequence for the latent map.} In the original, latent distances conflate
offset- and shape-differences; in the mixed kernel offsets are routed through channel (b), so
latent distances measure shape similarity more purely --- expect tighter, more interpretable
level clusters when comparing latent plots across the two models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Documented deviations from botorch, and why they are necessary}

\subsection{Every new parameter carries a prior}
lvgp-bayes's multistart (\texttt{GPR.reset\_parameters},
\texttt{models/gpregression.py} L143--149) \emph{samples restart initializations from the
registered priors}; a prior-less parameter is silently never restarted, and MAP has no
regularization on it. botorch leaves its \texttt{ScaleKernel} outputscales and categorical
lengthscales prior-less because its own fitter does not need priors. We therefore add, using
the package's own conventions:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{raw\_outputscale} \sim \mathcal N(0,1)$ on all three \texttt{ScaleKernel}s
(the prior \texttt{GPR} itself uses, L99--101);
\item $\mathrm{raw\_lengthscale} \sim \mathrm{MollifiedUniform}(\ln 0.1, \ln 10)$ on the
sum-part latent lengthscale $\ell_z$ (the prior lvgp-bayes puts on every learnable
lengthscale, \texttt{models/lvgp.py} L208--210).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reparameterization of the Hvarfner lengthscale constraint}
botorch declares \texttt{GreaterThan(0.025, transform=None)} --- the raw parameter \emph{is}
the lengthscale --- because botorch's fitter enforces constraint bounds inside the optimizer.
lvgp-bayes's \texttt{fit\_model\_scipy} optimizes raw parameters \textbf{unbounded}
(\texttt{optim/mll\_scipy.py}: \texttt{bounds=None}). Verified failure mode: L-BFGS walks the
raw lengthscale negative and the LogNormal prior's support check raises \texttt{ValueError},
killing every restart silently (the model is then returned at its initial values). Fix: the
\emph{identical induced model} --- same prior on the lengthscale value, same hard floor ---
reparameterized per the package convention as
$$\ell = 0.025 + e^{\mathrm{raw}},\qquad \mathrm{raw}\ \text{unconstrained.}$$
Residual edge case, discovered in the 30-seed campaign: a prior draw below $0.025$ during a
restart makes gpytorch's bounds check raise, and \texttt{fit\_model\_scipy} calls
\texttt{reset\_parameters} \emph{outside} its per-restart exception handler, so one bad draw
($P \approx 0.2\%$) kills the entire fit (observed once in 210 cells).
\texttt{LVGPRMixed.reset\_parameters} therefore overrides the sampler to clamp such draws
just above the floor.

\section{Considerations and caveats}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identifiability / over-parameterization.} The sum of two scaled parts plus the
latent's own scale freedom is redundant in ways MAP priors must arbitrate (botorch has the
same redundancy plus \texttt{Standardize}). In practice restarts converge to consistent optima
(e.g.\ camel $n{=}25$: two of three restarts at the same posterior value).
\item \textbf{Conditioning.} The additive structure can bring the kernel matrix closer to
singular; gpytorch occasionally adds jitter up to $10^{-3}$ during fitting (observed on
friedman $n{=}80$). Fits succeeded; monitor if reused at larger $n$.
\item \textbf{Gauge.} Fixing the \emph{product}-part latent lengthscale at 1 preserves the
original LVGP's identification of $\mathbf z$; the sum part's $\ell_z$ then measures how much
\emph{longer-range} the offset-sharing is than the shape-sharing. Fitted example (camel
$n{=}25$): $\ell_z = 1.66$ --- offsets shared more broadly across the latent space than
shapes, exactly the intended division of labor.
\item \textbf{HMC path unsupported.} \texttt{LVGPR.to\_pyro\_random\_module} assumes the
covariance tree is \texttt{ScaleKernel(Product(...))}; the MAP path used by the study does not
touch it.
\item \textbf{Interpretation of the latent map.} $\mathbf z$ is still a 2-D level embedding,
but distances now encode shape-similarity (product channel) with offset-similarity carried
separately (sum channel) --- when comparing latent plots against the plain LVGP, expect
tighter clusters, since offsets no longer inflate latent distances.
\end{itemize}

Across 30 noise realizations the mixed-kernel model takes the \textbf{best MAE in 5 of 7
configurations} (both Branin budgets --- including $n{=}25$, where it beats the MATLAB
H-LVGP's flagship result --- and all three Friedman budgets, by 22--25\% over the Categorical
GP) and the \textbf{best interval score in all 7}. The Categorical GP retains camel's MAE.
Coverage sits between 0.86 and 0.99 everywhere. The friedman result is the notable one: the
first configuration family where a latent-variable model beats the Categorical GP
\emph{decisively and replicably} at every budget --- evidence that once the offset channel is
provided, the latent geometry is an asset rather than a tax, exactly the mechanism the
study's griewank/camel arcs identified.
